When high-ability managers are scarce, cross-border movement of managers may contribute to high quality management. Using Hungarian administrative data on foreign-acquired firms and their CEOs from 1990 to 2019, we estimate that hiring an expatriate CEO raises total factor productivity by 8.5%, and sales by 118% relative to hiring a local CEO. We identify three reasons for sending expatriate CEOs: better general management skills, implementing parent-firm processes and facilitating exporting, and acting as guardians for the foreign owners' investment. We use an industry-equilibrium model to decompose the revenue gain into these three channels, attributing 87% to higher productivity, 10% to capital deepening, and 3% to improved export market access. Productivity gains are similar across tradable and nontradable sectors, pointing to general management skill as the main source of value. Capital deepening declines as Hungary's institutions strengthen, reducing the need to safeguard capital.
